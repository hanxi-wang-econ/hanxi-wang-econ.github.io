    - <small>Presented at CUHK Economics Department Alumni Academic Conference 2025, Journal of International Economics Summer School Poster Session 2025, CHUK-Tsinghua Macroeconomic Workshop 2025, Zhejiang University School of Economics 2025, Shanghai University of Finance and Economics College of Business 2025, CUHK-CUHKSZ Economics Summer School 2026</small>

    - <small><strong>Abstract:</strong> We derive optimal trade policies in a multi-country, multi-sector general-equilibrium (GE) model that encompasses a broad class of supply-side specifications. Unlike in partial-equilibrium models, the supply-side specification determines endogenous GE interdependencies across sectors and countries. In the two-country case, under our defined Generalized Independence of Irrelevant Alternatives (GIIA) Supply System, cross-sector interdependencies are simplified: although a policy in one sector affects supply in every sector, symmetric scaled cross-sector responses cancel from relative policy conditions. We obtain simple, explicit import-tariff and export-tax formulas that depend only on the targeted sector's structural parameters and relative market shares. With multiple countries, trade among foreign countries adds cross-country interdependence: the foreign trade network reallocates Home's aggregate sectoral net-import motive across foreign countries. We show that instead of depending solely on bilateral trade shares, network effects compress or amplify cross-country tariff differentials under different specifications. Using trade data, we show that ignoring these GE interdependencies can reduce Home's welfare gains.</small>
